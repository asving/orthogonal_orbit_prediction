Hi Sultan,

Thanks for the note about label-free recall and invariants. Here is how we found the attention mechanism, the order in which we checked it, and what we still don't understand. The repo contains only the final mechanism and the causal tests; this note traces how we got there. Our setup is the simplest version of yours: one system per sequence, no labels, no interruptions (6×6 Haar U, unit-norm v, 24 tokens).

**1. Hypothesis first.** Before training anything we wrote down two candidate algorithms. One estimates U by pseudoinverse and applies it, which needs 36 numbers in the residual stream and a matrix-vector product. The other works in token space: write x_t = Σ c_k x_k by least squares and predict Σ c_k x_{k+1}. That is an attention pattern (query x_t, key x_k copied forward by a previous-token head, value x_{k+1}) and never forms U. For a uniform prior over orthogonal matrices it is also the Bayes-optimal predictor.

**2. Behaviour.** A 2-layer model follows the least-squares error curve, including the drop to zero at n+1 tokens. Before n tokens it matches the pseudoinverse prediction sample by sample, not the truth, with all its error in the directions the context cannot yet determine. One layer fails, as expected if a previous-token head must compose with a later head.

**3. Heads.** Layer 0 has a previous-token head. Layer 1 has a sign-split pair of heads whose attention logits fit x_tᵀ W x_{j-1} with W a pure multiple of the identity (cosine ±1.000) and whose output maps are ±identity on x_j. The pair exists because softmax weights are non-negative and regression coefficients are not. Probes find U and the posterior-mean estimate of U nowhere in the residual stream, while the predicted next vector is decodable. The model's "knowledge of the system" is the context, read by attention.

**4. The correction we had missed.** When we checked whether attention weights equal the least-squares coefficients, they did not: they sit between c and the raw dot products s. The gap is exactly the pseudoinverse, since c_j = ⟨x_{j-1}, S⁺ x_t⟩ with S the 6×6 context covariance. So the question became how a network applies S⁺ without computing it. Fits pointed to a truncated Neumann series: layer-0 "similarity" heads write S x, layer-1 attention is S-preconditioned, and its output fits a fixed polynomial in S. In deeper models, later layers carry values of the form x_j minus the model's own earlier prediction of x_j, which is one gradient-descent step per layer.

**5. Causal tests** (repo `causal.py`). (i) Perturbing a token only where it is read as a value gives each pair's causal weight; in deeper models the weights added by later layers correct layer 1's shortfall toward c. (ii) Patching the layer-0 similarity heads at the query with a donor that has the same current token but a different context hurts badly and shifts the prediction in the direction first-order preconditioning predicts; the previous-token head, patched as a control, does not. (iii) Replacing layer-1 attention with an idealised dot-product pattern loses most of the accuracy; an S-preconditioned pattern recovers about two thirds of it.

**6. What we don't understand, and where your invariants come in.** A third of the attention pattern is a per-query adaptive correction we have not identified. And the mechanism does not generalise neatly: exact least squares works for any linear system, but our models fail even on orthogonal U with |v| = 0.5 or 2. The predicted direction stays roughly right (cosine 0.8–0.94) while the output norm stays near 1. So the network has learned norm conservation as a prior rather than an equivariant algorithm. That is close to your point: the whole mechanism runs on inner products, which U preserves (⟨x_i, x_j⟩ depends only on i−j), and the norm is one such invariant. We also checked the more explicit invariant route, the characteristic-polynomial coefficients of U (Cayley–Hamilton gives x_{t+n} = Σ a_k x_{t+k} with n fixed scalars). They are not decodable, so our models do not use it. Your interrupted-recall setting may favour a different solution. Training our setup with interruptions and no delimiter tokens would tell us whether recall reuses these regression heads or builds an invariant-matching circuit. We'd love to compare notes.
